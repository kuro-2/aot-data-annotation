What we do

So, what is data annotation?

It's the work of taking raw text, images, audio, video or records and labeling them against rules everyone has agreed on. AI teams use those labels to train their models and to find out how well the models actually perform.

A single label is usually small. Here are four kinds we write all the time.

Text

What was the searcher after?

"cheap flights to tokyo next week"

intent: travel bookingdestination: Tokyo

Audio

Who is speaking, and when?

speaker A at 0:03speaker B at 0:12

Video

When does the action start?

Document

Human in the loop

Where people come in

Software can do a lot of the labeling. Some calls still need a person. Our reviewers fix mistakes, compare model answers side by side, and flag anything subjective or sensitive.

They judge every item against the accuracy, fairness and safety criteria agreed for your project. Their feedback doesn't get thrown away either. It becomes examples your team can use to fine-tune how the model behaves.

Capabilities

What we can deliver

We work across six kinds of data. The review standard is the same for all of them.

Text and search

Search query intent, relevance ratings and summary evaluation.

For retrieval-augmented generation (RAG), reviewers check whether each answer is actually backed by the sources it pulled. For reinforcement learning from human feedback (RLHF), they rank responses and write down why they preferred one over another.

Audio and speech

Checking automatic speech recognition (ASR) transcripts, labeling speakers and reviewing captions.

We also listen to text-to-speech output for pronunciation and clarity.

Video and Physical AI

Labeling actions and objects, with the exact time each event starts and ends.

When there's more than one camera, we check the labels agree across every view.

Images and documents

Checking optical character recognition (OCR), text and character recognition, and transcribing handwriting.

When we pull a field out of a document, we link it to the page it came from.

Multimodal AI data

Question answering over images, audio or video, with reference answers tied to the evidence.

We also judge generated images against the text instructions they were made from.

Coding and expert evaluation

Python tasks that come with a reference solution and tests you can run.

Specialists write the scoring criteria and review the model's responses themselves.

    Getting started

    How a pilot runs

    Four steps, from looking at your sample to running at full volume.

    1. 1

      Define the pilot

      We look at a sample together and agree the output format, the label rules, the tricky edge cases and what counts as accepted.

    2. 2

      Pick the contributors

      Automated skill checks give us a shortlist. Human reviewers then try a task that looks like the real project and agree on how it should be scored.

    3. 3

      Set up the tooling

      Missing fields, duplicate records and bad timestamps get flagged automatically. Anything odd goes to QA, and approved batches are converted into the format we agreed.

    4. 4

      Scale up once you're happy

      We track accepted volume, turnaround and rework. Any error that keeps showing up goes into the guidelines before the next batch.

    Lessons from step 4 go back into step 1
